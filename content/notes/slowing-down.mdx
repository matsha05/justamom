**Hey there,**

I’ve started running again. I wasn’t always a runner. When I first started in
college, I could barely run a mile, but slowly I began to build up. I found a
kind of peace in it. The steady motion of my feet hitting the pavement.
Catching those first morning rays. Seeing how I could push my body faster and
farther. I ran in almost every country I traveled to and loved getting the lay
of the city before anyone else. Scoping out the little cafés and markets I
wanted to visit later.

Three kids and a few injuries later, I’m starting out again. A lot slower than
I used to be, but running nevertheless.

There is this whole running philosophy that, to improve your endurance, you
have to run slowly. It feels so counterintuitive. I’m shuffling my way down the
road, trying to keep my heart rate down, wondering why it can be so exhausting
when I’m barely moving faster than a walking pace.

It’s uncomfortable and awkward, revealing all the ways my body is tight or
stiff or sore. I can’t distract myself with speed or simply power my way
through the rest of the run.

If I’m honest, slowing down in life is also incredibly uncomfortable.

There is this idyllic morning I have in my mind where we have no plans.
Everyone lingers around the breakfast table, then runs off to play or read
books on the couch, relishing the openness of the morning.

In reality, those mornings with nothing on the calendar look more like breaking
up sibling fights, dealing with big emotions, pestering everyone to finish
their chores and trying to breathe through the mess creeping its way across my
counter. Putting up with “I’m bored!” and “He’s looking at me!” My first
reaction is to get out of the house.

But what if the point of slowing down isn’t to have the perfect Instagram
morning, but instead to notice the heartbeat of my home? The needs that surface
as boredom and bad attitudes. My own impatience rising as the noise grows and
the mess spreads. Maybe those things were always there, but I was just moving
too quickly to notice them.

I love how Jesus never seemed to hurry or rush from place to place. In Mark 5,
Jesus is on His way to the home of Jairus, whose daughter is dying. This need
is clearly urgent. The crowd is pressing around Him as He walks, and in the
middle of the commotion, a woman reaches out to touch His clothes, desperate
for healing. Jesus could have continued walking. He could have healed her
without ever acknowledging the interruption. But instead He stopped. He saw
her.

I am sure that to everyone around Jesus, this looked like a distraction from
the mission. After all, a daughter was dying! But Jesus was not worried because
He knew that His Father had a plan. He could be interrupted. He could take this
time to heal and connect. He called her “Daughter” and listened as she told Him
her story.

When I slow down, I become aware of every tight muscle and uneven breath. In my
home, I begin to notice the quiet cries for attention beneath the rude words.
The discomfort beneath the complaints. The weariness beneath my own impatience.

The answer isn’t always to sprint past it or hustle everyone out the door.
Sometimes the answer is to stop. To notice. To listen. To pay attention to what
the slower pace has revealed. It may not look peaceful or productive. It may
not look like much at all. But maybe this is how endurance is built, one slow
step at a time.

In it with you,

Lizi
