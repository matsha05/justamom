Some days, It feels like we’re all slowly tumbling towards chaos. Our health is declining, our jaws are shrinking, our morality is questionable, and it is never my fault, but always his fault and her fault and the earth is getting hotter and the ice caps are melting but we’ll need an ice pick to stay the course on this slippery slope.

It feels like the state of our world is a weight too heavy to bear. We’re drowning in waste, plastics are seeping into our pores, the population is shrinking yet children seem a sacrifice too great a cost. We’ll have them but only if we can choose when and where and how many. As long as we can keep our careers, chase our goals, hold onto self.

It’s like as the dust collects and our bodies break down, entropy takes me by the hand and pulls me along in its grasp. Everything is crumbling and disintegrating and fading and down we go as I toss another pair of holey socks, wipe the dust off the mantle, watching wrinkles form and knees grow weak wrapping my heart in despair.

And yet, there is a grip that is firmer and stronger. That grasps the thread of my heart as it starts to unravel and holds me fast. Though my body wastes away, it reaches into the depths of my mind and whispers, “this is not the end”. That anchor lodged in my soul forces my gaze upwards and fixes my eyes on something else that looked a lot like death: the cross. This device of torture that was meant for evil, He used for good.

We wear them around our necks, tattoo them on our bodies and hang them up in our homes. This symbol of destruction that now represents hope. Peace. Reconciliation. It reminds us of victory. That we do not end in the grave. We can stand in the tension of the already but not yet. For sin may have entered the world through one man and death through sin, but how much more did God’s grace and the gift that came by the grace of the one man, Jesus Christ, overflow to many!

And so I’ll let go of entropy’s hand and grasp the hope that does not disappoint. I can laugh at another gray hair, shrug my shoulders at the growing mess on my counter, hold my children close and teach them about this wide wonderful world that the Lord has made.
